Pip and the Giggling Castle

Pip was a small green dragon who lived in a warm cave near an old castle on the hill. Every evening, the torches on the castle walls glowed gold. Flick, flick, flick. Pip loved toexplore when the torches flickered.

Pip had tried to fly once. The first flight was very wobbly. Flap, flap, wobble, wobble, bump! But Pip was not sad. Pip just tried again, one flap at a time.

One night, a breeze whispered past Pip's ear. "The castle has a hidden treasure room," it said. "Find it before the moon is high!"

Pip's eyes grew wide. "A treasure room!"

Pip hurried to the castle. Inside, the halls were full of echoes. Pip's footsteps went tap, tap, tap, and the echoes went tap, tap, tap. A door went creeeak. Pip's heart went thump, thump.

Pip took one careful step. Then another. Then another.

"I will sneak in through that tall window," Pip whispered. Pip climbed up, up, up. Pip wiggled through the window. But then Pip's nose began to twitch.

"Ah... ah..." Pip tried to hold it in. "AH-CHOO!"

CLANK! A suit of armor jumped and rattled.

Then the armor sneezed too. "AH-CHOO!"

Pip stared. The armor stared. Then they both started to giggle. Pip giggled so hard that Pip tumbled right off the windowsill. Flump! Pip landed on a soft pile of pillows.

"I'm Mo," said the armor, in a squeaky little voice. "The dust tickles my nose."

"I'm Pip," said the dragon. "My nose tickles too!"

They giggled again, until their tummies hurt.

Then a painting on the wall gave a big wink. It was Lady Lumen, a lady with a tall, feathery hat.

"Hello, friends," she said with a twinkle. "Solve my riddle, and the treasure room door will open. I have hands, but I cannot clap. I have a face, but I cannot smile. What am I?"

Pip scratched a little green head. "Hands? Hmm. Hands that cannot clap."

Mo tapped his metal chin. "A face with no smile?"

Pip thought one careful thought. Then another. "Wait! Maybe it is something that tells time?"

"Tick-tock!" said Mo.

"A clock!" they shouted together.

Lady Lumen laughed and clapped her painted hands. "Yes, a clock! You solved it together!"

With a creak and a rumble, the treasure room door swung open. Inside, a shelf sparkled with glowing lanterns. Next to them sat a big jar of honey drops.

"Oooh," whispered Pip.

Pip and Mo shared the honey drops. They were sweet and golden and warm.

The torches began to dim, down to a soft, quiet glow. Flick... flick... flick... slower and slower.

Pip gave a big yawn and curled up beside Mo's shiny armor. Mo's arm made a cozy blanket over Pip's wings.

Somewhere deep in the castle walls, a gentle hum began. It was a lullaby, low and soft, just for them.

Pip's eyes grew heavy. Mo's voice was a whisper now. "Goodnight, Pip."

"Goodnight, Mo," yawned Pip. "Tomorrow, another careful step."

The castle hummed on, and the torches glowed low, and two new friends drifted off to sleep.
